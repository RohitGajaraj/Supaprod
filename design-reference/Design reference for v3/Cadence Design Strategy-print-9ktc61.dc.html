01

Product understanding

Cadence is not a PM tool with AI features. It is a decision and outcome operating system: a swarm of governed agents runs the product loop (sense, decide, define, build, ship, learn) and the human makes only the calls that matter. The moat is not the build, which commoditizes. It is the compounding, auditable record of what you decided and whether you were right.

Three truths from the repository that every design decision must serve:

Truth 1 · Attention is the product
The user's scarce resource is judgment. Every pixel either routes a decision to them or gets out of the way. A screen that merely displays information is a failed screen.
Truth 2 · Trust is earned pixel by pixel
Autonomy is only sellable if every agent action is cited, observable, gated, and reversible, and the interface makes that legible at a glance instead of burying it in an audit log.
Truth 3 · The moat must be visible
Compounding memory is the defensible asset, yet today it is invisible. The user must regularly see "this learning moved these priorities", or the moat might as well not exist.

Who it serves: the individual senior PM first (the daily evidence-to-decision ritual), the product team second (shared memory, governance), the VP as buyer (the accountability record). The interface must feel personal at the front and institutional underneath.

02

UX audit · why it feels scattered

Findings are grounded in the code, not impressions. The product has real power (250+ capabilities, a genuinely deep engine). The failure is structural, and it repeats in seven patterns:

F1 Feature accretion, no spine 40+ authenticated routes (inbox, briefing, cockpit, discovery, fleet, impact, delegate, meetings, memory, drift…) accreted one at a time. The nav has already burned through four metaphors. Features landed where there was space, not where intent lives.
F2 No shared object model The same mission renders differently on Today, Missions, Build, and Govern. A decision is a card here, a row there, a toast elsewhere. Users can never transfer learning between screens, so every screen must be re-learned.
F3 Attention is scattered "Needs you" lives in at least five places: nav badges, an Engine Room badge, approval cards on Today, gates inside mission detail, and banners. The one thing the product exists to route, the call, has no single home.
F4 Today is a dashboard, not a ritual The current Today page is a collage of cards: vitals, pulse, priorities, bottlenecks, brief, agents rail, tasks. A PM's morning is three questions (what needs me, what changed, is the machine healthy) and none is answerable in one glance.
F5 Power is hidden, not revealed 250+ capabilities communicate as clutter or not at all. There is no progressive disclosure model: everything is either on-screen at once or undiscoverable. Onboarding is disconnected from the surfaces it should teach.
F6 AI is a place, not a presence Chat is a destination you travel to. In an AI-native product, intelligence should be summonable in context, on the object you are looking at, not a separate room where context is lost.
F7 The language is machine-first Buttons say what the engine does ("Delegate to OpenHands", "Run dispatch"), not what the human means ("Build this"). Labels expose internal mechanisms. Every piece of copy must be rewritten from the user's side of the glass.
03

The design idea

Cadence is a calm instrument: a dark, quiet cockpit where the machine's work glows softly in glacier, and the only thing that ever asks for your attention, in ember orange, is a decision that genuinely needs a human.

Everything follows from three laws. They are the answer to F1–F7, and they are what makes 250 features feel organized instead of scattered:

1
One object, one anatomy
Everything in Cadence is one of seven objects: Signal, Opportunity, Spec, Mission, Call, Outcome, Learning. Each has exactly one card, one detail view, one status language, identical wherever it appears.
2
One queue for attention
Every gate, approval, and decision request is a Call, and all Calls live in one queue with one badge. Ember orange is reserved exclusively for it. Clear the queue and the interface is visibly, satisfyingly quiet.
3
Depth on demand
Three layers everywhere: a quiet list, a slide-over panel, a full view. The 250 capabilities live at layers two and three. Layer one never shows more than one decision's worth of information.

Organized by intent, not by feature. The PM's day has five intents: decide, discover, plan, build, learn. The navigation is exactly those five, plus AI summonable everywhere and governance behind one door. No feature ever gets its own nav item again; it joins the intent it serves.

06

Screen-by-screen recommendations

TodayRebuilt
Weak because: a passive card collage; the PM's actual morning questions are unanswerable in one glance. Proposal: a ritual in three acts. Act one: the Call queue, full-width, one call at a time, keyboard-first (approve, send back, open). Act two: What changed overnight, each row naming the cause ("this learning re-ranked these two bets"). Act three: one line of machine status. Nothing else. Principle: a screen with one job; recognition over recall. Impact: the daily habit becomes a five-minute ritual with a visible "all clear" finish line.
DiscoverRebuilt
Weak because: signals and opportunities are tabs of tables; evidence and judgment are disconnected. Proposal: a two-pane evidence desk. Left: the live signal feed, clustered by theme, each signal a verbatim quote with its source. Right: the opportunity queue ranked by ICE, each with the Critic's verdict chip inline; selecting one shows its evidence trail in place. The Critic teardown ("Challenge this") is a first-class action on every opportunity, and the packaged first-run wow. Principle: keep evidence attached to judgment; make the wedge feature impossible to miss.
PlanReorganized
Weak because: specs and roadmap hide inside Product; docs float in a separate module; committing has no ceremony. Proposal: specs as living documents (serif body, citations in the margin, the Critic's stress-test beside the text) and a three-column roadmap where every bet declares its outcome measure at commit time. Placing a bet into Now is a deliberate act with a confirmation that states the promise being made. Principle: commitment deserves ceremony; documents deserve typography.
BuildMerged
Weak because: Missions and Build are two screens for one intent, with duplicate mission renderings and controls named after the machinery. Proposal: one cockpit. A quiet list of running, waiting, and finished missions; a slide-over with numbered agent steps, the live step pulsing in glacier; the gate inline when a mission waits on you; the raw trace one toggle deeper; cost and model in the footer of every agent message. External build tools are an implementation detail: the button says Build this, and the engine picks its own hands. Principle: observability without vertigo; name the outcome, hide the mechanism.
MemoryElevated
Weak because: the moat is a filing cabinet: memory, learnings, decisions, docs as flat lists no one visits. Proposal: a record with consequences. Decisions carry their outcome verdicts (validated, missed, pending) and every learning shows what it moved ("re-ranked Usage alerts +1.1"). The belief graph is browsable. The PM's personal track record (calls made, hit rate, saved spend) is exportable, their portable career asset. Principle: the moat must be felt weekly, or it does not exist.
OnboardingRebuilt
Weak because: disconnected from the product; teaches nothing about the loop. Proposal: onboarding IS the first teardown. Pick your track (solo PM, founding PM, tech founder), connect one source or use the seeded workspace, then point Cadence at a feature you believe in. Ten minutes later the Critic's cited red-team lands on your Today, and the product has taught its loop, its evidence model, and its wedge in one stroke. Every later capability introduces itself contextually, the first time its trigger appears, never as a tour. Principle: the first win is the onboarding.
Engine RoomRestructured · 10 tabs become 4 rooms
Weak because: ten flat tabs (controls, approvals, guardrails, budgets, prompts, evals, analytics, gauntlet, traces, drift) with equal weight and no story — things were parked where they fit. Proposal: the door opens on a health summary — one verdict line per room, plus the aurora loop-health score — and the ten tabs regroup into four rooms named after the user's question, not the mechanism:

06·B

The user journey · making 250 features speak

The founder's diagnosis is exact: power that is not visible does not exist. The answer is never to show more at once — it is a product that always knows your next step and introduces itself as you go. Five mechanisms:

1 · The golden path
Day zero is scripted to one outcome: sign in, pick your track, connect one source (or take the seeded demo workspace), point the Critic at a feature you believe in. Ten minutes later a cited teardown lands on Today — and the user has touched the whole loop without being taught it. Every day after, Today opens with the same three beats: your calls, what changed, all clear.
2 · The loop strip · the product explains itself
SENSE · 128 → DECIDE · 3 CALLS → DEFINE · 2 → BUILD · 4 → LEARN · 1
A persistent strip on Today shows the loop as a living object: real counts at every station, the live pulse where agents work, ember where you are needed. Click any station to land inside it. The user absorbs the product's architecture by glancing at it every morning — no tour required.
3 · The next-step engine · nothing dead-ends
Every object detail ends with one suggested action, chosen by the engine: NEXT · TURN THIS INTO A SPEC — and every empty state is an instruction with a time estimate ("No signals yet · connect Intercom, takes 2 minutes"), never a blank box. The user never has to ask "now what?"
4 · Contextual reveal · features introduce themselves
A capability appears exactly when its trigger first exists: the first failed outcome introduces rollback; the tenth signal introduces clustering; the first approved spec introduces "Build this". One quiet glacier coach mark, one line, dismissed forever. Power is revealed by use, not by a 12-step tour on day one.
5 · The catalog · "what can it do?"
One honest answer to the 250-feature problem: a searchable capability index inside ⌘K. Every capability has a plain-words name, a one-line pitch, and a "Try it" that performs it on your real data. A short "new tricks" digest lands weekly. Depth stays hidden until asked for — then it is one keystroke away.

Full-saturation ember never colors a label — it is reserved for elements that act or ask. The AI shimmer: text and marks that represent the machine actively working may carry the slow living gradient (glacier → cobalt → violet → orchid → ice, 5s drift — the register Gemini made familiar, with real depth in the darks); static labels never shimmer, so motion in color always means live intelligence — and at most one element per screen shimmers, ever. Caramel was retired from the ramp — it read like an old-age platform; Melon takes its slot. The restraint budget (per screen): at least 90% of any screen is neutral — canvas, ink, hairlines; one ember CTA; at most one aurora card; at most two pencil annotations; status color only on actual status. Color earns attention because it is rare. One machine voice: glacier is THE machine's color; royal violet exists only inside the shimmer gradient and the working butterfly, never as a standalone accent anywhere else. The grayscale test: every screen must still make complete sense with color removed; if a meaning lives in color alone, add the word.

09

Motion principles

Motion means something is alive
Only three things ever move on their own: the live pulse on a working agent, the progress of a step, and the arrival of something new. Decoration never animates.
One easing, three durations
cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1) at 140ms (controls), 200ms (panels), 280ms (page transitions). Entrances are transform-first with staggered reveals of 30ms per row, capped at six rows.
Panels slide, pages settle
Slide-overs enter from the right with a soft backdrop; surface switches crossfade-and-rise 8px. Approving a Call collapses it with a brief moss confirmation before the next call rises. The reward for clearing the queue is the interface going perfectly still.
Respect the off switch
All motion gates on prefers-reduced-motion and an in-product toggle. Live state falls back to a static ring, never disappears.
10

AI interaction model

AI appears in exactly three postures, so the user always knows what mode the machine is in. Never a mascot, never a floating orb, never "✨ magic".

Ambient
It already did the work
Drafts, rankings, clusters, and summaries appear pre-made with a quiet "by agent" mark and their sources. You edit, you never start from blank.
Summoned
⌘J on anything
A side panel over the current screen, pre-loaded with the object in view. Ask, challenge, rewrite, compare. Close it and you are exactly where you were.
Delegated
Missions with gates
Multi-step work runs as a mission: observable steps, cited hops, a gate wherever it touches the world. You watch in Build, or just answer the Call when it comes.

The trust contract, everywhere: every AI output carries its sources; every action states its consequence in helper text; every gate is reversible; the machine speaks in glacier and asks in ember. The user should feel "it understands what I am trying to do" because the AI always arrives holding the right context, not because it talks a lot.

11

Accessibility

Contrast, verified
Body text #B5AFA6 on #0A0A0B passes AA (7.9:1); primary text #F2F0ED passes AAA (13.9:1). Ember and glacier are reserved for accents and always paired with text or shape, never the sole carrier. Mono metadata never falls below 9.5px with wide tracking.
Keyboard-first, by design
1–5 switch surfaces, ⌘K acts, ⌘J summons, A/S answer the current Call, Esc always closes the top layer. Focus is a 2px glacier ring, visible on every interactive element. Slide-overs trap focus and return it. Hovering any control reveals its shortcut as a quiet mono hint, and every interactive element has designed hover, active, and focus states: a slight lift, a brightened hairline, a soft glow in the element's own role color. Nothing is clickable that doesn't visibly answer the cursor.
Meaning survives without color or motion
Every status pairs a word with its dot; verdicts are text chips, not colored dots alone. Reduced motion swaps pulses for static rings. Live regions announce Call arrivals and mission completions to screen readers.

Brand, naming, logo

The name is open. "Cadence" stays as the placeholder, but it describes rhythm, not judgment, and it sounds like a hundred other SaaS names. The winning name should do what Lovable did: a warm, human word nobody expects on enterprise software, that instantly says what the product believes. Candidates, in ranked order:

HunchRECOMMENDED
The PM's gut, finally with receipts. Warm, five letters, impossible to forget, and the whole thesis in one word: you bring the hunch, the machine brings the evidence. "Hunch red-teams your roadmap."
Good Call
The product literally routes calls to you. Every approval confirms with "Good call." The brand smiles without a mascot. Two words is the only tax.
Instinct
Premium and confident: instinct, trained on evidence. Reads enterprise-safe while staying human. Slightly colder than Hunch.
Compound
Names the moat: judgment that compounds. Investor-legible, strategic, but head over heart.

The mark: the Butterfly stays. New concepts were explored and rejected by the founder. The original bilateral butterfly returns exactly as drawn, translated to the dark palette, and is promoted from decoration to status instrument: still when idle, a slow flutter while agents work, an ember glow when a call is waiting.

The Butterfly · elevated
The original warmth, gradient-lit: molten ember upper wings, saffron-gold lower wings (the production pairing), a fine light edge, porcelain body. No ring, no halo — just a deep shadow and the faintest ember cast, so the mark sits on the canvas like an object. The arrival: it enters the way you described — wings spread wide, a short flight across the corner, four quick beats, then it lands and settles into the slow two-wing rest (replaying live above; the same choreography is the product's splash and loading moment).

The status states
Each state has its own halo, and the glow intensity is the signal: idle wears quiet ash wings with the faintest pearl ring, working glows a breathing royal violet, and a waiting call burns the brightest ember halo. Color and intensity tell the story before you read a word. The favicon follows the same states.
Standing bar for any future mark that hopes to replace it: one idea drawn in one gesture, meaningful (not generic geometry), legible at 16px, capable of the three status states, and never anything that reads as a spinner or a chart. Until something clears that bar, the Butterfly is the identity. The wordmark stays bold lowercase with an ember full stop, whichever name wins.

Voice & microcopy · standing rules

The product speaks like a sharp PM, not like software: calm, declarative, contractions welcome, and one wink per screen at most. It uses the user's own vocabulary (backlog, scope, ship, bet, roadmap) and saves the humor for safe moments. Trust moments — calls, failures, money — are always plain and calm. The humanized-output law (hard rule, product-wide): no em or en dashes anywhere (use the middot, a comma, or a new sentence), no invisible Unicode characters, no AI-cliche words (seamlessly, leverage, empower, robust, unlock, delve), no exclamation marks, no emoji. This applies to every string in the interface AND to everything the agents generate for users, enforced by the output sanitizer. Buttons follow the plain-words law (section 08); titles are two to four words; the one emotional word per screen is set in Newsreader italic.

Future scalability strategy

The current mess came from having no rule for where feature 251 goes. Now there is one, and it is mechanical:

The placement algorithm · for every new feature
1Which of the seven objects is it about? It renders as that object's anatomy, or it extends the anatomy for everyone.
2Which of the five intents does it serve? It lives inside that surface. It never gets a nav item.
3Which layer earns it? Glanceable state at layer one, working detail in the panel, full depth in the full view. Default is one layer deeper than you think.
4Does it need attention? Then it is a Call in the one queue. No new badges, banners, or dots, ever.
5Is it rare-but-needed? It lives in ⌘K and the object's context menu, discoverable by search, costing zero pixels.

This scales across the roadmap: team features arrive as presence on the same objects (who made the call, whose memory), enterprise arrives as depth in the Engine Room (SSO, audit, residency), the MCP/interop story arrives as objects readable by other agents, and new agent faces join the same mission anatomy. The design system never has to be rethought to grow; growth has a place waiting for it.

Standing instructions · for any builder, human or AI
This document is the brand contract. Claude Code, Lovable, or any agent building a feature reads it first and obeys these laws without being asked:
1Run the placement algorithm (section 13) before writing a line. New features never get new nav items, badges, or banners.
2Colors come only from the role colors and the named ramps (section 07). Role colors carry meaning, ramps carry data. Never invent a hex.
3Buttons are plain human words with the consequence in helper text. No mechanism names, ever.
4Copy follows the voice rules (section 12): PM language, one wink per screen, calm at trust moments, and the humanized-output law — no em or en dashes, no invisible Unicode, no AI-cliche words, in the interface or in anything agents generate.
5Anything that needs the human is a Call in the one queue. One home per concept; no input is ever asked twice.
6Design every state before shipping: hover (lift, brightened hairline, role-color glow), focus, active, empty (an instruction, never a blank), loading, and error. A shortcut for every repeated action, revealed on hover.
7Motion and glow only where something is alive or needs attention, one easing, three durations, gated by reduced-motion. Gradients only as aurora score washes.
8Every object renders with its one canonical anatomy (section 03). If a new view of an object is needed, extend the anatomy for everyone.
9Obey the restraint budget (section 07) on every screen: 90% neutral, one ember CTA, one aurora card, one shimmering element, two pencil annotations, and glacier as the only standalone machine accent. Before shipping, run the grayscale test — the screen must make complete sense with color removed.
