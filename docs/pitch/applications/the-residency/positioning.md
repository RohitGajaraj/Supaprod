## The single most important thing about this application

**They explicitly ban links, and they tell you why.**

> _"please don't include links except where we specifically ask. part of what we're evaluating is how well you can explain your work without leaning on external references."_

Every other application we have written leans on `supaprod.ai`, the brief deck, and a demo login. **All of that is unavailable here except in the fields that specifically ask for a URL.** The prose has to do the entire job.

Practical consequences:
- Never write "you can see it at" or "as shown in the deck".
- Concrete nouns and numbers instead of a link: not "our approval queue" but "a queue of decisions an agent made overnight, each one opening to the evidence it used".
- The form says **"be concise"** on five separate questions. Take that literally. Short beats complete here, and long answers read as someone who cannot compress their own thinking.

## What The Residency actually selects for

It is a live-in house for people who build, not a fund. That changes the rubric:

1. **Are you actually building right now?** Not planning, not researching. Shipping.
2. **Are you interesting to live with for twelve weeks?** The roommates question is not filler. They are assembling a house.
3. **Do you think for yourself?** The "one thing only you believe" question is the highest-signal field on the form and the one most people waste.
4. **Trajectory over credentials.** The accomplishments question is scoped to the **past three years**, which deliberately discards anything older.

## Our honest fit, including the part that works against us

**Working for us:**
- He is genuinely building, alone, right now, which is the exact thing this program exists for. Evidence it by shipped features and a working end-to-end system, **not** by commit count: see doctrine Rule 6.
- The three-year accomplishment window suits him: it covers the Intellect AI platform work and all of Supaprod.
- He is open to five of their locations including San Francisco and New York.
- The contrarian-belief question is a gift for someone with an actual thesis.

**Working against us, and it is real:**
- **The Residency skews young.** Its reputation is built on backing very early-career builders, often college-age. A founder with a decade of industry experience and an MBA is atypical for the house. This is not disqualifying, but we should not pretend it is not there.
- **No money, and it costs money.** This is not funding. It is a room, a community, and a deadline. Worth applying to as optionality, not as a funding strategy.
- **Cohort dates collide.** 7 Sept to 29 Nov overlaps both the September public launch and the South Park Commons bootcamp (late Sept to late Nov). If SPC lands, they cannot both happen.

**How the answers handle the age question:** they never mention it. The accomplishments answer **opens** on the change ("two years ago I could not have shipped a line of this"), which reframes a decade of experience as velocity rather than seniority, and the tone stays plain because a corporate register would confirm the concern instantly.

**Why each accomplishment point does a different job.** With zero users the artifact cannot carry an achievement claim on its own, so point 1 claims the **capability** and uses the system as evidence for it, which is unimpeachable. Point 2 carries the **credential**, the only item with real users and real revenue. Point 3 carries the **cost**, leaving the AVP title. Three points, three jobs, no overlap. An earlier draft had points 1 and 3 both claiming the transformation.

## The location question

He currently has San Francisco, New York, Bangalore, Ithaca and Munich selected. **Keep all five.** Selecting more locations raises the odds of placement without weakening anything, and the form asks which you are *open to*, not which you prefer. Under the US-primary ruling San Francisco and New York are the goal; Bangalore is the no-visa fallback; Ithaca and Munich are pure optionality.
