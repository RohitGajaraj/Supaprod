## Eligibility: resolved, and in our favour

EF's marketing page says *"Europe's next founders"*, which looked like a hard bar. **The Bridge's own site says something broader:**

> _"For **non-US founders** at the very beginning: pre-idea, pre-team, or just getting started."_

Non-US, not European. He qualifies. The Europe framing is positioning copy, not the gate. His TUM MBA and two and a half years at Infineon in Munich also give him a genuine European claim if it ever comes up.

## The real risk, and it is the whole application

**EF invests in talent before ideas. He has a company.**

Their own language for this programme is "pre-idea, pre-team, or just getting started". He is nine weeks into a working product with 4,876 commits, a live beta and a September launch. On EF's stated intake he is past the stage they buy at.

**The resolution, and it is the same move that fixed the Residency accomplishments answer:** do not present Supaprod as the thing being funded. Present it as **evidence of the talent**, which is what EF is actually purchasing. He is not asking them to back a product. He is demonstrating, with a working system, the thing their whole thesis is a bet on: that one exceptional person can now build what a team used to.

Framed that way, being further along stops being a mismatch and becomes proof. Framed the other way, a reviewer thinks *wrong programme*.

## EF's published rubric, and how he scores

From EF's own guidance (Alice Bentinck, co-founder) and their assessment framework.

### The concept they care about most: your EDGE

> _"What are the things you know that no-one else knows? Most applicants don't understand what their edge is and get distracted telling us about new skills and knowledge that they plan to acquire."_

**His edge, stated as things he knows that others do not:**

1. **What it is actually like to be the single human in the loop.** Nine weeks of directing an agent fleet against a production codebase, including the failure he learned from: approving everything until he became the queue. Almost nobody has run that experiment on themselves yet.
2. **How AI behaves in regulated production.** Three years shipping AI into banking, where "the model decided" is not an accepted answer and a name is attached to every outcome.
3. **Why surfacing does not change behaviour.** He built the dashboard version first and watched it fail. That is a diagnosis he owns rather than a thesis he read.

**Do not** let the answer drift into what he plans to learn. EF says explicitly that is the common mistake.

### Behaviour: challenging convention, drive to achieve, followership

| Criterion | The evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **Challenging convention** | Threw away four complete working versions. Left an Assistant Vice President title to build alone. Chose not to compete on code generation while everyone else raced there. |
| **Drive to achieve** | 4,876 commits in nine weeks, solo, seven days a week. 401 features specced, 362 shipped, independently audited. |
| **Followership** | **This is the one applicants fumble.** He has led cross-functional teams of 20+ engineers, designers and ML specialists at Intellect, and coordinated 20+ engineering teams plus NASA, ESA and JAXA at ISRO. That is verifiable evidence people follow him. |

### Ability: he is a CEO profile with unusual technical depth

EF splits ability into Technical Understanding plus Commerciality (CEO profile) or Valuable Tech Knowledge plus Applicability (CTO profile). He is the first, but with a technical floor most CEO applicants do not have: a 500-test evaluation suite with LLM-as-judge and deterministic graders, six frontier models A/B tested in production, inference cost cut 35% at 99.2% accuracy.

**Say it once, with the numbers, and move on.** Overclaiming CTO-profile depth invites a technical interview he does not need to sit.

### Their bluntest line, worth obeying

> _"Saying you built something is stronger than saying you care."_

Every answer leads with a built thing.

## The other EF tracks, and why we are not applying to them

Four are open. Separate portals, no published rule against applying to more than one, but EF's FAQ says to contact them if unsure which fits, and their US process explicitly runs interviews "to determine fit and the right pathway for you".

| Track | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **The Bridge, SF** | **This one.** SF, non-US founders, visa help, best terms. |
| London Program (Aug 4) | **Skip.** Same organisation, wrong geography under the US-primary ruling, no visa help, three-day scramble. |
| XF2 Experienced Founders, London | Skip. London again. |
| US Funding & Fellowships, SF | **Mention it, do not separately apply.** Rolling, SF, $250K day one. Better stage fit on paper. Raise it in one line to EF rather than filing a second application, so the routing is their call and he does not read as scattered. |

**Standing decision:** one strong application plus a short note saying he is open to whichever US pathway fits. That gets the same coverage as double-applying, without the scattered signal, and it creates a human contact.
