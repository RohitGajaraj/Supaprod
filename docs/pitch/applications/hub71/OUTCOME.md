## How to read it, because reading it wrong is the known failure here

**The five bullets describe what the SHORTLISTED startups had. They are not an assessment of Supaprod.** The letter never says which we missed. It is a template sent to everyone not shortlisted.

**This board's standing rule applies and is not suspended by the bullets existing:** recording an invented reason is worse than recording none, because a later session reads it as signal and moves the positioning against something nobody said. That rule was written after South Park Commons. It holds here.

**What IS new, and it is genuinely useful:** this is the first of three rejections that states criteria at all.

| | Programme | What they said |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-08-11 | South Park Commons | *"not the right fit"* — a volume statement |
| 2026-08-21 | EF The Bridge SF | *"highly competitive... only a small group can progress"* — a volume statement |
| **2026-08-24** | **Hub71** | **five named criteria** — a checklist |

**A checklist is worth more than a reason.** A reason applies to one filing; a checklist can be held against every future one, which is what the rest of this file is for.

## The one structural finding that is safe to act on

**Bullets 2 and 4 select on demonstrated traction and an implemented solution at scale.** Our own recorded research on this programme — the `Why it fits` field of the Notion card, written before filing — says Hub71 *"explicitly sets no minimum traction, revenue or MVP requirement."*

**Both are true at once, and the gap between them is the finding.**

> **A published "no minimum traction" is a statement about ELIGIBILITY. It is never a statement about SELECTION.** It tells you the form will accept you. It tells you nothing about who gets shortlisted out of an oversubscribed pool, and an oversubscribed pool is selected on evidence.

That is a reusable correction to how this board reads programme pages, and it does not require guessing why we were rejected.

**Second, recorded and not concluded:** bullet 3 says *"(co-)founders"*, *"collective expertise and diverse skill sets"*. The `Solo founder` field on this card reads `Yes` — the programme publishes no two-founder rule. **The letter suggests team composition carries weight in selection even where it is not a gate**, which is the same shape as the traction finding.

## What must be collectable before the next application, and how

Derived from their five criteria. **Each row is a thing we must be able to SHOW, not a thing to write more persuasively about.** The distinction is the point: bullets 2 and 4 both ask for evidence.

| # | Their criterion | What we must be able to show | Where it comes from | State today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A critical, widely prevalent problem | The problem stated in one sentence, plus how many teams have it and how we know | [`../../verified-numbers.md`](../../verified-numbers.md) | Have the sentence; the prevalence number needs a source that is not our own assertion |
| 2 | Evidence the implemented solution is effective **and scales** | A working product a stranger can use, and a before/after with the query behind it | The live product + [`../../verified-numbers.md`](../../verified-numbers.md) | **The gap.** Every number we cite must survive the numbers rule; a number without its query is not evidence |
| 3 | Founder–problem connection, and **skills across the team** | The decade in regulated finance, stated as qualification; and an honest answer on team composition | [`../../founder-story.md`](../../founder-story.md) | Founder connection is strong. Team breadth is the open one |
| 4 | A stage that can use the programme, **demonstrated progress** | What shipped, dated, with what it changed | Git history and the tracker | Have the record; it is not assembled into a progress narrative anywhere |
| 5 | Why THIS programme and THIS city, specifically | A named local reason: an institution, a regulation, a customer, a person | Per programme | **Strongest answer we filed.** The AI-native government strategy and the ADGM/FSRA sandbox leg are specific and verifiable |

**Read row 5 with the others.** Our Abu Dhabi case was specific and it was not enough on its own, which is consistent with an oversubscribed pool selecting on rows 2 and 4 first.

### The rule this adds to the drafting procedure

> **Before filing anywhere, answer this in one line each: what is the evidence, and can a stranger check it?**
>
> If the answer is a claim rather than something checkable, it is row 2 and row 4 exposure. Fix the evidence or file knowing the gap — but do not fix it by writing the claim more confidently, which is what an application form invites.

## Two live doors this leaves open

- **Reapplication, in their words:** *"We encourage you to carry on building, and apply again in the future."* Cohort 21, whenever it opens.
- **ADGM setup, offered independently of the programme**, at `bd@adgm.com`. **This does not depend on Hub71 saying yes.** The Abu Dhabi leg of the thesis — the AI-native government strategy, the FSRA sandbox — is reachable without the cohort, and this is a founder call rather than an application task.

## What did NOT change because of this

**The positioning.** Three rejections across three programmes with three different filters, one decided in seven days, is not evidence the thesis is wrong. The discipline recorded on the master tracker after the second rejection stands: fix what we can prove is wrong, and leave the thesis alone until something actually says it is wrong. **Nothing in this letter says it.**
