## Where we win here, in the order to spend their attention

**1. The team bar is the whole game, and we have the unusual answer to it.** CF asks for "at least one tech and one business co-founder". Most solo applicants answer that by apologising or by claiming the missing half. **Rohit holds both halves with credentials in each**: BE in Mechatronics and flight-hardware engineering at ISRO on the tech side, TUM MBA and close to a decade of product at Infineon and Intellect on the business side. That is not a workaround for their requirement, it is a literal satisfaction of what the requirement is trying to buy.

**2. The German card, and it is the one most applicants cannot play.** TUM School of Management MBA. Product manager at Infineon Technologies **in Munich**. Coming to Baden-Württemberg is a return, not an arrival. [`../positioning-doctrine.md`](../positioning-doctrine.md) Part 3 already rules this the lead for the German ecosystem: *"Lead as a returning TUM alumnus with a shipped deep-tech product. Infineon Munich makes the German connection real rather than opportunistic."*

**3. The B2B regulated-industry card, which is exactly the Heilbronn buyer.** The Intellect platform is used by 200+ financial institutions across 70+ countries. The first question a regulated buyer asks about agents is not what the model can do, it is who answers for what it did. The Schwarz-backed German industrial and B2B corporate ecosystem around Heilbronn feels agent governance as a live problem, not a future one.

**4. The build story clears their stage bar with room.** They want a working MVP beyond prototype with early signs of traction. The product runs end to end, is in private beta, and a reviewer can open a login and walk the whole loop.

## Where we lose here, and the counter

**A. Solo, against a stated two-founder preference.** See win 1. Do not apologise, do not over-explain, do not volunteer it before the form asks. State it once as both-halves-carried, then move on.

**B. "Early signs of traction" is the phrase we are thinnest against.** Private beta, invite-only, public launch mid-September. **Never volunteer the zero** ([`../how-to-draft-the-next-one.md`](../how-to-draft-the-next-one.md) rule 0). The counter is the working login plus dogfooding as first-party evidence.

**C. The exclusivity clause and the December end date.** CF bans parallel accelerator participation and runs 30.09 to 18.12, which collides with EF The Bridge SF in October and with the US-primary ruling.

> **Founder ruling 2026-08-16: apply anyway, say nothing.** It restates the standing doctrine (*"Hub71, Station F, Munich. Apply anyway. They cost only drafting time, they are free optionality, and a competing offer is leverage"*). **The clause binds participants, not applicants**, and nothing on the form asks. If an offer lands, the conflict is a good problem and it gets decided then.

**D. Deep tech, on a page that names AI, Cybersecurity and Robotics.** We are AI and B2B, so we are in scope, but the framing must be **agent infrastructure and governance**, never "a product management tool". Same instruction the doctrine gives for a16z Speedrun.

## The two things that make this application non-generic

The quality gate ([`../positioning-doctrine.md`](../positioning-doctrine.md) Part 5, test 6) requires at least two things that would be **wrong** in an application to a different programme. Here they are, so a later reader can check they survived editing:

1. **The both-halves answer written directly against CF's "one tech and one business co-founder" wording.** It would be off-key at EF, which evaluates individuals by design, and at Antler, which forms teams inside the programme.
2. **The Germany return, TUM plus Infineon Munich plus Baden-Württemberg.** It would be irrelevant at every US programme on the queue and actively wrong at Hub71.
