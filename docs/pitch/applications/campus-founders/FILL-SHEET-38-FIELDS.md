**17b. What role does AI play within your solution?** _(Required — NOT in the left rail, discovered on the live form 2026-08-16)_

_Their hint: "It is improving the quality of predictions, finding new relationships in data, etc."_

```
AI is not a feature inside Supaprod. It is the workforce, and a person keeps the judgment.

Agents run every station of the product lifecycle. They read the signals a team already has, cluster them into opportunities using vector retrieval over pgvector, and a critic agent argues against the weak bets before anyone commits. Agents then write the spec with its evidence attached, plan and design the work, hand the build to coding agents that open real pull requests, ship, and write the release notes. When the result lands, a verdict agent grades what shipped against what the spec promised.

Every model call goes through one runtime chokepoint that handles budget, cache, guardrails, tracing, fallback and feature gates, so models are interchangeable parts rather than a dependency. Retrieval vectors run on Cohere embed-v4. The agent loop runs on Qwen-plus and Gemini 2.5 Flash, with GPT-5 used where reasoning depth earns its cost. A better model is a same-day drop-in at no engineering cost. Agent-written code executes in E2B sandboxes with secret redaction on every output stream, so a token cannot reach a log.

On prediction quality specifically, which is what your question asks about. At the moment a decision is made, the team's forecast is captured: what they expect, how they will know, and by when. Those fields are made immutable by a BEFORE UPDATE trigger in Postgres, so not even our own agents can revise a prediction after the outcome is known. The system brings each forecast back when it falls due, drafts the verdict from what actually shipped, and files it against the decision that caused it. That graded history is what re-ranks the next set of bets. The predictions get better because they are scored, not because the model got bigger.

Two things AI deliberately does not do. It cannot cross the merge gate: a person approves and merges, and that is the only place a human is required. And it does not compute performance scores about named human teammates, which is a design decision rather than a gap.
```

> **This field answers their hint directly in paragraph four.** They ask about "improving the quality of predictions"; the honest mechanism is that predictions improve because they are **scored**, not because the model grew. No claim of accumulated learning in the present tense.

**31. Why Heilbronn?** _(Optional, "Reasons for joining an accelerator program in Heilbronn over others")_

```
Three reasons, and the first is about a deadline that has already passed.

On 2 August 2026 the EU AI Act's transparency duties began to apply. Article 12 requires automatic logging over a high-risk system's lifetime. Article 14 puts a duty on the provider to build the system so a human can genuinely oversee and halt it. Article 26 lands the matching duty on the deployer, which is the German company running the agents, not the vendor selling them. Supaprod was built around that shape before it was a legal one: a merge gate no agent can cross, an audit trail on every agent action, a stop the customer can reach in one action, one-key rollback on anything an agent produced, and a deliberate refusal to score named human teammates, because Annex III point 4 makes AI used in task allocation and performance evaluation high-risk and I would rather design that exposure out than document it. A US-built product tool is not designed around any of this. Heilbronn is where the buyers who feel it live, with IPAI next door and the Schwarz Group, Bosch and Audi in the region.

Second, I am coming back rather than arriving. My MBA is from TUM School of Management, and TUM Campus Heilbronn sits on the same Bildungscampus as Campus Founders. I was a product manager at Infineon Technologies in Munich. I have built product inside German engineering culture and I know how a German industrial buyer evaluates, so Baden-Wuerttemberg is not an unfamiliar market to me.

Third, the thing I am genuinely short of. The honest risk in this company is distribution, not the product. I built the engine before opening the doors, which for a product whose value comes from decisions graded against outcomes was the right sequence, because an empty system teaches you nothing. It was also the wrong sequence for learning fast, and it means I have had far less contact with users than someone eleven weeks in should have. Twelve weeks onsite with access to German B2B corporates is a direct answer to that specific gap, which is why this programme and not a generic one.
```
