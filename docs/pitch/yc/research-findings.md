## 1. The rules from YC itself

### 1.1 Logistics (verified)

- **Fall 2026 deadline: July 27, 2026, 8pm PT.** Decisions by Aug 28; interviews (mostly virtual) Aug–Sep; batch Oct–Dec in San Francisco. $500K standard deal ($125K for 7% + $375K uncapped MFN SAFE). Four batches/year. "Applying early is strongly encouraged because it gives the partners more time to review your file and gives you time to provide meaningful updates." — https://www.ycombinator.com/apply
- **Re-applications:** "In a typical YC batch, about half the companies applied multiple times before being accepted… Having made progress since your last application is a strong signal to us." — https://www.ycombinator.com/faq. Dalton Caldwell: progress between applications and addressing prior feedback is "heavily weighted." — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B5tU2447OK8
- **Pre-traction is normal:** "On average, 40% of the companies we fund in each batch are just an idea," and "Most don't have any revenue." — https://www.ycombinator.com/faq
- **Solo founders:** "Yes. We regularly accept solo founders." ~10–11% of recent batches (22 solo in W26). — https://www.ycombinator.com/faq · https://www.ycombinator.com/library/7P-does-yc-fund-solo-founders · https://www.extruct.ai/research/ycw26/
- **Numbers get verified:** "if you state numbers in the interview we may ask for verification of them afterwards." — https://www.ycombinator.com/interviews

### 1.2 Writing rules (PG, Dalton, Seibel, Tan, Epstein)

- PG, "How to Apply": "Whatever you have to say, give it to us right in the first sentence, in the simplest possible terms." / "We're immune to marketing-speak; to us it's just noise." / the most important question is the impressive-thing-built one / "print it out and take a red pen and cross out every word you don't need." — https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply
- Dalton: "The first thing I look at when I read an application is the team." / "I look for something that is clear and well written. I don't like buzzwords. Conciseness. Clarity. Bluntness." / reader test: can he retell your application as a story (who, backstory, what exists TODAY, evidence of want, evidence of seriousness)? / "Intentional misrepresentation = automatic disqualification." / cites GitLab's application as the clarity model. — https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/27/how-to-get-into-y-combinator-dalton-caldwell/ · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B5tU2447OK8
- Michael Seibel's five elements (his claim: all five → >50% chance): technical founders who can build; pre-existing founder relationships; progress relative to time; insight others ignore; communication skills. "Explain it like you would to your grandmother." — https://picampus.it/getting-into-y-combinator-according-to-its-ceo-michael-seibel/
- Garry Tan (read 6,000+ applications): "Teach the reader something new as fast as you can." / show-don't-tell, plain English, inverted pyramid (conclusion first). — https://www.ycombinator.com/library/J7-garry-tan-s-tips-for-applying-to-yc · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wbCVN1yLyA
- Aaron Epstein (8,000+ applications): "One take, no editing." Write for "a very tired YC alum." — https://medium.com/hackernoon/how-to-make-your-yc-application-stand-out-1781a716f7b1

### 1.3 Videos (official)

- Founder video: **1 minute**, "nothing except the founders talking," "Do not recite a written script: Use bullet points instead." "Statistically we're much more likely to interview people who submit a video." — https://www.ycombinator.com/video
- Demo: include one even if unpolished — "the difference between nothing and something is actually huge" (YC's Stephanie Simon). — https://www.ycombinator.com/library/J8-yc-application-tips-include-a-demo
- Community norms: 60–90 seconds of demo attention, one "magic" feature; funded videos "often look terrible… but they sound incredible - dense, factual, and fast." — https://zyner.io/blog/yc-application-video-guide · https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-application-tips-2025

### 1.4 The RFS trail (Supaprod's demand signal)

- Summer 2026 RFS: **"Company Brain"** (Blomfield — "a living map of how a company works"; "an executable skills file for AI… every company in the world will need one"), **"The AI Operating System for Companies"** (Diana Hu — "make your entire company queryable"; turn the "open loop into a closed loop"), **"Software for Agents"** (Epstein — "the next trillion users… will be AI agents"), plus "SaaS Challengers." — https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs (fetched 2026-07-10; verbatim pulls in [`../../research/investor-corpus-yc-vc.md`](../../research/investor-corpus-yc-vc.md))
- Earlier: Andrew Miklas's YC essay **"Cursor for Product Managers"** — the category-naming piece; Supaprod's six founding threads (recorded 2026-06-03 in [`../../archive/idea-origination-inputs.md`](../../archive/idea-origination-inputs.md)) are literally RFS categories, thread #1 being this essay. Our operator's critique of it: [`strategy/archive/v9-decision-wedge-and-build-next.md`](../../strategy/archive/v9-decision-wedge-and-build-next.md).
- Garry Tan on RFS intent: "YC wants founders who treat AI agents not as features but as the core operating system of brand-new companies and industries." — https://x.com/garrytan/status/1920153493492674984

### 1.5 The 2025–26 shift: AI-built codebases are now legible to YC

- Jared Friedman: ~25% of W25 had codebases "95% generated by AI." — https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/06/a-quarter-of-startups-in-ycs-current-cohort-have-codebases-that-are-almost-entirely-ai-generated/
- Garry Tan (Uncapped #43): "For the first time, for the spring batch, you can upload a transcript of your Codex or Claude Code making a feature… You can tell a lot about whether someone can build just from how they prompt the agents. Do they know systems?" Harj Taggar: "Do they use plan mode?… Are they over-engineering?" and "The Parker Conrad of today is just in Claude Code." What matters: "agency and taste." — https://uncappedpod.com/p/y-combinator-in-the-age-of-ai-ep
- The counterweight, Diana Hu: "you have to have the taste… to know that an LLM is spitting bad stuff or good stuff." — https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/06/a-quarter-of-startups-in-ycs-current-cohort-have-codebases-that-are-almost-entirely-ai-generated/
- **No sourced YC-partner quote exists saying they detect/penalize LLM-written applications** (searched official channels 2026-07-10). Do not cite one. The operative rules are Dalton's anti-buzzword/anti-generic lines and the unfakeable-artifact shift (transcripts, demos).

## 2. The seven deadly sins (and the community anti-pattern corpus)

### 2.1 The video: "How My Startup Made it to Y Combinator After 7 Failed Attempts" — Steli Efti (Close), Aug 2025

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c31sCXaz-Xo — 7 rejections over 7 years, then accepted. His seven deadly sins, from the transcript:

1. **No track record** — nothing built/shipped before; YC-founder recommendations helped his accepted try.
2. **Not building anything substantial** — "PowerPoint presentations and business plans… no MVP." "If you're not a builder, you can't make it into YC."
3. **Haven't talked to enough users** — prove constant user contact (PG's first act on meeting him: made him enroll a paying user that day).
4. **No surprising insight** — "people want more features" signals superficial thinking; the insight must be earned and unintuitive.
5. **Not moving fast enough** — his accepted app read as a momentum timeline: "a month ago idea → a week later MVP → two weeks later first 10 paying users → press → 250K angel check."
6. **No proof of resilience** — YC backs "cockroaches" (the Airbnb cereal story).
7. **Sounding like you won't make it without YC** — neediness is disqualifying; his accepted application already had product, customers, momentum.

Runner-up video (different creator, adjacent lessons): "Why YC rejected me 7 times" — Anthony Sistilli, Nov 2025 — sub-$1B TAM caps you; pivoting between application and interview is a red flag; idea-hopping across batches is a red flag; modern YC weighs traction over credentials. — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WLwNXWodf1s

### 2.2 Top 12 anti-patterns (ranked, sourced)

1. Vague/obfuscated writing — the reader can't retell it as a story (Dalton, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B5tU2447OK8)
2. Buzzwords instead of plain English (Tan, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wbCVN1yLyA)
3. Misrepresented traction/revenue — automatic disqualification (Dalton, ibid)
4. LOIs / "committed pilots" passed off as traction (CommandBar self-grade, https://web.archive.org/web/20240111013321/https://www.commandbar.com/blog/grading-commandbars-yc-application/)
5. Decks and plans but nothing built (Close sin #2)
6. No user conversations, no surprising insight (Close sins #3–4)
7. Not full-time / seriousness gap (HN, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41987512)
8. Technical product without demonstrated ability to build (Dalton; Carranza, https://miguelcarranza.es/yc-application — note §1.5 updates what "technical" means in 2026)
9. Sub-$1B TAM with no path to $100M/yr (Sistilli; Tan)
10. "We need YC to succeed" neediness (Close sin #7)
11. Novel-length answers, ignored video directions, sloppy grammar/units (Dalton; Carranza)
12. Idea-hopping between batches / pivoting between application and interview (Sistilli)

### 2.3 Top 10 rewarded patterns (ranked, sourced)

1. A story-shaped application: who you are, what exists today, who uses it (Dalton)
2. Sub-50-char one-liner naming the thing in plain words (CommandBar; GitLab via https://zyner.io/blog/gitlab-yc-application)
3. First sentence = the whole answer; proof after (Tan; CommandBar: "Make sure your first sentence of each answer is how you'd answer it if there was a word limit")
4. Specific numbers everywhere, however small (GitLab; Dropbox, https://www.ycombinator.com/apply/dropbox)
5. Candor about stage — CommandBar answered "How far along?" with "Not far." (anti-selling beats inflation)
6. A compressed momentum timeline (Close sin #5 inverted)
7. One surprising, earned user insight (Close sin #4 inverted)
8. Seriousness evidence: progress since last application; re-applying is a plus (Dalton; YC FAQ)
9. Demoable product the partner can click (Dalton; YC library J8)
10. Named competitors with a mechanism-level critique (GitLab: GitHub Enterprise = "black box VM that doesn't scale… 4x more expensive"; Dropbox: existing tools are at "the wrong abstraction level")

## 3. The landscape you're applying into

### 3.1 Batch composition (agent-saturated)

| Batch | Size | AI/agent share |

| W25 | ~160 | ~80% AI-focused (https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/13/10-startups-to-watch-from-y-combinators-w25-demo-day/) |

Implication: "AI agent for X" is the most crowded pattern in YC history — differentiation must be structural (the evidence/outcome record), not adjectival.

### 3.2 Funded neighbors (partners will pattern-match against these)

- **Fission AI (W26)** — plan mode for complex features. https://www.extruct.ai/research/ycw26/
- **Glen (W26)** — unified org context for agents and humans; solo founder. https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/glen

- No dedicated "AI product manager OS" company found in F25/W26/Spring 26 — the seat is open.

### 3.3 Solo / India / relocation

- Solo ≈ 10–11% of recent batches; solo applicants "must show stronger early traction or technical capability." — https://zyner.io/blog/yc-solo-founders
- Solo non-technical precedent: Deepak Chhugani (Nuvocargo, W18). — https://medium.com/did-you-know-the-journal-blog/yc-as-a-solo-nontechnical-founder-2424c5c25e25
- India: YC-backed Indian startups fell from 66 (2021) to ~4 (2024); the accepted profile is Indian founders building for global markets + relocating. — https://operator.blog/2025/03/10/why-is-yc-backing-relatively-fewer-indian-startups/

### 3.4 Funnel math (calibration, not discouragement)

~7% of applications get interviews (partner Kathrina Manalac via https://zyner.io/blog/yc-acceptance-rate); ~40–45% of interviews convert (https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/yc-acceptance-rate); overall 1–2%, reportedly under 1% in 2025 (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sadiasaifuddin_the-acceptance-rate-for-y-combinator-this-activity-7371218381842264067-8B7d). The written application is the biggest single filter.

## 4. Getting in with zero traction (the playbook)

- **Lago (S21)**, accepted pre-product: lived expertise (Qonto growth), "formally interviewing another 100+ Growth leaders," 70+ inbound contacts before a landing page, weeks from first code to application. "You're executing and iterating fast" + "you know something unique about your problem space." — https://getlago.com/blog/how-we-got-into-yc · https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33582002
- **Blacksmith (W24)**: applied ~2 weeks in, no product; leaned on backgrounds + market conviction. — https://www.blacksmith.sh/blog/going-through-yc-winter-24
- **Greptile (W24)**: rejected once, accepted next cycle — a clean rate-of-progress story. — https://research.gatech.edu/y-combinator-backing-and-30m-investment-take-startup-greptile-next-level
- **Replit**: rejected 3x ("just a fun toy") before acceptance; organic traction finally beat pitch polish. — https://replit.com/blog/yc
- **Formidability** (PG): "A formidable person is one who seems like they'll get what they want, regardless of whatever obstacles are in the way… roughly justifiably confident." Don't imitate swagger: "you'll just end up in an uncanny valley." — http://pencerw.com/feed/2013/9/19/paul-graham-on-formidibility
- **Slope over intercept**: YC interview guide — "the best way to get an edge is to work hard and have your startup improve between the time that you applied and the time that you interview." — https://www.ycombinator.com/interviews
- **The user-contact bar** (Eric Migicovsky's canonical lecture): the only two jobs are "code or build your product and talk to users"; discovery = extract data, never pitch ("Tell me about the last time you encountered that problem"; "What, if anything, have you done to try to solve it?"). If prospects aren't already hand-rolling solutions, "the problem isn't burning enough." — https://medium.com/@davegoldblatt/startup-school-reference-guide-for-lecture-3-how-to-talk-to-users-with-eric-migicovsky-f4afd41b8e9d
- Seibel: "Never ask users for features… The user's job is to give you problems." — https://x.com/StartupArchive_/status/1806293924371366267
- Honest pre-launch phrasing shape: "prototype live, 15 target customers interviewed, 6 said they'd pay." — https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-application-tips-2025

## 5. How winning applications are written (language forensics)

### 5.1 Accepted-application excerpts (verbatim)

- **Dropbox (S07)**: "Dropbox synchronizes files across your/your team's computers. It's much better than uploading or email, because it's automatic, integrated into Windows, and fits into the way you already work." / "kind of like taking the best elements of subversion, trac and rsync and making them 'just work.'" / lived-detail humor: "'proposal v2 good revised NEW 11-15-06.doc' continue[s] to crack me up." — https://www.ycombinator.com/apply/dropbox
- **GitLab (W15)**: "We're making open source software to collaborate on code." Numbers do the work (100K+ orgs, ~$1M run rate, ~60% monthly growth). — https://zyner.io/blog/gitlab-yc-application
- **Cruise (W14)**: two weeks in, no prototype — hard constraints instead ("under $3,000," "only solving 90% of driving scenarios"). — https://www.getintoyc.com/company/cruise/
- **OpenPhone (W18)**: 50-char "Phone system meets CRM built for small businesses"; bank balance stated exactly ("34000"). — https://www.quo.com/blog/openphone-y-combinator-application/
- **Paystack (W16)**: "12 Pilot merchants and up to 400 on the waiting list," revenue "$1,300." Founder-fit in speech rhythm: "Well, we're firstly developers, then business owners, and merchants…" — https://www.getintoyc.com/company/paystack/
- **CommandBar (S20 self-grade)**: 50-char states the problem ("Make web apps easier to navigate and learn"); answered "How far along?" with "Not far."; warns pseudo-traction ("only one of those 4 ever even created an account… some usage is better than hypothetical usage"). — https://web.archive.org/web/20240111013321/https://www.commandbar.com/blog/grading-commandbars-yc-application/

Common signature: sentences of 8–20 words, first person, present tense, named competitors, exact numbers however small, one flash of personality, weaknesses stated as facts with a mitigation.

### 5.2 Partner before → after rewrites

1. "We are going to transform the relationship between individuals and information." → state the product in sentence one (PG: it "sounds impressive, but it conveys nothing").
2. Mission-statement opener → PG's rewrite: "A database with a wiki-like interface, combined with a graphical UI for controlling who can see and edit what."
3. "Revolutionizing the world of digital communication via the social web" → "a mobile application showing you nearby restaurants your friends recommend" (Harj Taggar, https://www.quora.com/What-is-the-best-advice-for-a-startup-applying-to-Y-Combinator).
4. "We're a marketplace for space" → "we allow you to rent out the extra room in your house" (Seibel, https://www.startuparchive.org/p/michael-seibel-on-how-to-create-a-great-startup-pitch).
5. "Disrupting wave prediction… bleeding-edge social platform… gamification and advanced machine learning" → "Accurate wave prediction for surfers." (https://razorpay.com/blog/writing-an-outstanding-y-combinator-application/)

### 5.3 Banned words and AI-tells

Marketing-speak (partner-sourced): revolutionize · disrupt · transform · bleeding-edge · cutting-edge · leverage · gamification · "advanced machine learning algorithms" · "AI-powered platform" · empower · unlock · seamless · abstract "marketplace for X" · mission-statement openers · vague superlatives.
AI-tell vocabulary (PG flagged "delve"; Pangram's list): delve · tapestry · vibrant · landscape ("ever-evolving") · navigating · testament · realm · embark · "it's important to note" · "when it comes to" · "not just X but Y" · rules-of-three · em-dash chains · tidy intro-body-conclusion shape. Human writing shows specificity and unvarnished observation. — https://www.technollama.co.uk/to-delve-or-not-to-delve-ai-detection-made-easy · https://www.pangram.com/blog/how-to-detect-ai-writing · https://andiai.com/blogs/announcement-yc-application-advisor-writing-reads-like-20260324-1519

### 5.4 Real 50-char one-liners

Airbnb "Book rooms with locals, rather than hotels." · Cruise "Self-driving cars." · OpenPhone "Phone system meets CRM built for small businesses" · Paystack "APIs and tools to enable merchants accept payments in African Markets" · Simple Habit "5-minute meditation app. Spotify for meditation." · Dendron "Superhuman for note taking" · Prolific "Quickly find research participants you can trust." — https://shizune.co/yc-application-examples/describe-what-your-company-does-in-50-characters-or-less

### 5.5 The 10-rule writing checklist

1. First sentence = the product, in words a parent understands.
2. One idea per sentence; most answers 1–4 sentences; red-pen every word.
3. First person, your own speech rhythm.
4. Numbers over adjectives, however small.
5. Name competitors; say what they get right before what they miss.
6. Weaknesses as facts + mitigation; never spin.
7. One known analogy, used once.
8. Delete every banned word and every mission statement.
9. Kill AI cadence; add one true, slightly imperfect detail only you could know.
10. One-liner: under 50 chars, noun-first, named user, zero buzzwords.

## 6. What this means for Supaprod (the synthesis applied in fall-2026-application.md)

1. **Lead the rollover with the delta** (§1.1 re-application signal) → the 8h "did anything change" field carries the momentum timeline.
2. **True-on-submit-day law** (numbers verified; misrepresentation = disqualification) → bracketed slots, two variants for "are people using."
3. **The Tan/Taggar shift makes the solo non-technical profile legible** → the who-writes-code answer + attached Claude Code transcript are load-bearing, not apologetic.
4. **The discovery-call count is the single biggest upgrade available before July 27** (Lago pattern; Close sin #3) → checklist item 1.
5. **Structural differentiation against a saturated field** → competitors answer names ChatPRD/Linear/Scott AI and wins on "none of them records whether the call was right."
6. **RFS vocabulary as confirmation, not origin** → decade-of-pain first, dashboard origin second, RFS echo third (founder ruling 2026-07-10).
7. **Videos redone to spec**: 1:00 founder (bullets, one take), ~2:15 demo (magic in first 30s, dense/factual/fast).
8. **Neediness scan + retell test** added to the pre-submit checklist (Close sin #7; Dalton's reading method).
9. **No claim of LLM-detection** — but write like Dropbox/Paystack anyway (§5.3).
10. **Odds honesty**: ~7% → interview, ~40–45% → accepted. The controllables are all in the checklist; the August lever is real users + visible slope by interview time.
