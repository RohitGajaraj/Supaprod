**On the closer, and why I did not use your words.** Your instinct is right that the mission line is not a
human sign-off, and the video needs one. But "looking forward to hearing from you" is the exact posture the
application research flags as the seventh deadly sin: nothing may read as *I need you to pick me*. The stance
that works is **this is happening; you are welcome to come along**. So the sign-off **offers** instead of
asking: _"I'd love to show you the rest of it. Thanks for watching."_ Same warmth, none of the asking.

## Your two questions, answered first

**"Does 'for ten years I've been a product manager' sound like bragging, or does it say I know product and
have lived the pain?"**

Neither. It is **inert**, which is worse than either. A flat tenure claim makes the listener do the work of
deciding what it means, and in a sixty-second video nobody does that work. Two fixes, both applied in v3:

1. **State it as time in the problem, not as a title.** "I've been in the product space for close to a decade now" is a
   timeline. "I'm a product manager" is a job description. You asked for exactly this in your last message and
   you are right: the timeline version says you were present for the change, which is the whole point.
2. **Make the decade pay immediately.** Tenure plus a lesson is credibility. Tenure alone is a CV line. So the
   decade is followed straight away by what it taught you, which is that the same question kept coming back in
   three unrelated industries.

**"It's about nine and a half years, not exactly ten."** Then say **"close to a decade."** It is true, it is
easier to say than a number, and it is the repo's own phrasing in the locked §9a answer. Never say "ten years"
if it is nine and a half. A partner who checks LinkedIn and finds nine and a half has caught you rounding,
which is a small thing that costs a disproportionate amount.

**"Before that I worked on space systems" is wrong.** Correct. The decade _contains_ space, semiconductors and
banking. v3 lists them as one arc inside the decade, not as a prelude to it.

**"Should I call out my education?"** One clause, optional, and marked as the first cut. An MBA is a
credential, not a story, and this video's job is the story. Where it does help: it explains the arc from
engineering to product without you having to narrate the transition. So it earns a subordinate clause and
nothing more. Say **"an MBA in Germany"** rather than naming the school. The school name buys nothing spoken
and the founder canon restricts institution naming on non-YC surfaces anyway.

## On length, honestly

The storytelling you asked for costs words. This script is **304 words full, 277 lean**, which lands roughly
**1:35 to 1:45** depending on your pace. That is over the 1:30 you named.

**My recommendation: shoot the lean and let it land at 1:35.** The accepted corpus supports it: 14 of 19
accepted videos run past 60 seconds, one accepted at 97 seconds, and YC's own showcased exemplar runs 2:37.
A tight, well-told 1:35 beats a rushed 1:25.

**But I have now guessed at your speaking pace twice and been wrong once, so measure it.** Read the lean
version aloud, seated, full voice, with the pauses, against a stopwatch, before you practise anything else.
If it lands over 1:45, drop to the TIGHT cut marked at the bottom.

>

> I'm **building** what was missing. /
> Agents that know what to build, / ship it, / and remember. ///
>
> It reads everything your company already knows / and tells you what to build **next**. //
>
> You say yes, / and agents write it, / design it, / build it, / and ship it. /
> **Nothing merges without you.** //
>
> And it **remembers**. / Every call, / and what happened after. /
> So next time it tells you what **worked**, / and warns you before you repeat what did **not**. ///

### Beat 6 · the proof (1:37 to 1:47)

> I've built it **solo**, in eight weeks. / `[CUT 4 →]` Over four thousand commits. `[← end CUT 4]` /
> One person directing agents. / Every change on the **record**. //
>
> It is **live**. / Public launch in **September**. ///

### Beat 7 · the close, then the sign-off (1:47 to 2:00)

> Agents do the **work**. / You **answer** for it. / **Supaprod is how you answer.** (down) ///
>
> (smile) I'd love to show you the rest of it. / Thanks for watching. (down)

**Do not change the three-sentence close.** The sign-off after it is new in v3.1 and is the only part of the
ending you may adjust. Say it warmer and slightly slower than everything before it, and let your face change.
It is the one moment in the video where you stop presenting and just talk to the person watching.

## Why each beat is shaped this way

**Beat 2 does the work your title claim was not doing.** "Three industries that have nothing in common, and in
all three, the same question every Monday" is the sentence that earns your decade. It converts tenure into
**pattern recognition**, which is the only form of experience an investor actually prices. And it is not
bragging, because the claim is about the job, not about you.

**Beat 3 switches to "you", and that is deliberate.** You asked for the pain to land on investors who are not
product people. The switch from "I" to "you" puts them in the chair. And the question itself, *"why did we
build this?"*, is the question **they** ask their own portfolio companies. So they are not learning about a
stranger's problem; they are recognising their own. That is why this beat is the longest one in the script.

**"You know there were good reasons. You just cannot find them."** This is the most important line in the
video and the one to slow down on. It is what makes the pain feel like competence being wasted rather than
someone being disorganised. Without it, a listener thinks "that person should take better notes."

**Beat 4 explains the commoditization in words a non-technical listener already believes.** "Building software
used to be the expensive part, now agents do it in minutes" needs no background. Then the turn: the hard part
got _harder_, not easier. That inversion is the insight, and it is why the company exists.

**Beat 5 never names a feature or a surface.** It describes three things happening to a person. The tagline
lands first, then each of its three clauses is unpacked in the same order, which is what lets someone retell
your company after one watch.

**Beat 6 has no adjectives.** Solo, eight weeks, receipted, live, dated.

**Beat 7 is untouchable.** Three sentences of 4, 5 and 6 words, repeating "answer" rather than reaching for a
synonym. The easiest line in the script to say under pressure, which is why it goes last and is rehearsed
first.

## Facts, and what stays out

Everything in v1's fact table still binds. These are the ones that changed with v3.

| Never say | Say | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "ten years" | **"close to a decade"** | It is nine and a half. Rounding up is checkable on LinkedIn. |
| "I'm a product manager" | **"I've been in product for close to a decade now"** | Timeline over title. Your call, and it is the right one. |
| "Before that, space systems" | **"Space systems, then semiconductors, then banking and finance"** | The decade contains all three. |
| "ISRO", "Intellect", any employer | nothing, or the domain | Your ruling. The work is the credential. |
| "TUM School of Management" | **"an MBA in Germany"**, or cut it | The school name buys nothing spoken. |
| "the company brain" | **"and it remembers"** | Not our phrase to claim as identity. Describe the mechanism. |
| "where the record lives" | **"it tells you what worked"** | Banned framing. Storage is not the product. |
| anything implying users | **"It is live."** | Zero outside users exist on the database. |
| "Supaprod is building Supaprod on its own" | **"one person directing agents"** | Claim-gated until it demonstrably runs. |

**The rename line makes this video application-specific.** Only the applications you filed as Cadence need it.
For any investor you approach fresh, cut Beat 1's second line and open straight into Beat 2. Everything else
in the script is venue-neutral, so one recording serves both with that single cut.
