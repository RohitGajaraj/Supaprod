## What kind of video this is

**A webcam talking head. Nothing else in frame.** YC, verbatim: _"The video should be 1 minute long and should
contain nothing except the founders talking."_ And: _"This is not the place to submit a demo or promotional
video."_ So: no slides, no screen share, no product shots, no logo animation, no music, no titles, no cuts.
You, sitting close, looking at the lens.

**It is a communication test, not a pitch.** YC: _"we want to hear how the founders communicate."_ The
question a partner is answering in 60 seconds is: would I back this person.

**It is a URL field, not a file upload.** Upload to YouTube, set visibility to **Unlisted** (not Private, or
the partners cannot watch it), paste the watch URL. So there is no file size limit and no re-encoding to do.
The form field reads: _"Please enter the url of a 1 minute unlisted (not private) YouTube video introducing
the founder(s)."_

## On length: shoot the 90-second version

You asked for one and a half minutes. Here is the honest picture, then the call.

**YC's stated rule is 1 minute.** It appears twice: on ycombinator.com/video and in the form field label.
And Dalton Caldwell says on YC's own channel that following the instructions is itself scored: _"our entire
application process has these detailed instructions and we definitely take into account if you follow them in
our selection criteria. This is one of the ways to tell how detail-oriented someone is."_

**But the accepted corpus routinely runs longer.** Nineteen accepted or YC-endorsed application videos,
measured with `yt-dlp` off the community playlist
(`youtube.com/playlist?list=PLK7HoSSSROuL9e1bdG_oGEo-jSPuOjw6X`) plus YC's own five exemplars:

> 46, 47, 50, 55, 59, 61, 61, 62, 63, 64, 64, 66, 74, 76, 77, 90, 91, 97, 157 seconds
>
> Median **64s** · mean **72s** · **14 of 19 exceed 60 seconds** · Artisan W24 accepted at **97s** ·
> and YC's own showcased exemplar, Zenefits W13, runs **157s (2:37)**.

**The call: shoot the 90-second version and aim to land at 85.** Three reasons. Your real risk is not
instruction-following, it is running out of air; a 110-word budget forces you to speed up, which is the exact
failure mode you told me you have. Eighty-five seconds sits inside the accepted range with room to spare.
And your currently attached video is **2:54**, so 1:30 is already a 50% cut and visibly responsive.

**The 60-second cut is below, marked.** If you want the instruction-following point, shoot that one. Both are
built from the same beats, so practising the long one gives you the short one for free.

## How to read the notation

| Mark | Means | What you physically do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/` | half-second pause | small top-up breath through the mouth |
| `//` | one to one and a half seconds | breathe in through the nose |
| `///` | two-second beat change | look away and back, or nod once |
| **bold** | the one stressed word in that sentence | punch it. **One per sentence, never two.** Two stresses is what makes delivery sound recited. |
| `(down)` | pitch drops | so the last line lands finished, not questioning |

Ten to twelve seconds of the 90 is silence. That is deliberate: **a pause immediately before a hard word
gives your mouth time to set up, and a pause after a fumble is heard as thinking, not as error.**

was the single biggest deviation from the corpus._

### Beat 2 · the one credential, fused into the story (0:12 to 0:30)

> I started at **ISRO**, / India's space agency, / at twenty-one. /
> Building systems where a mistake **cannot be undone**. //
>
> That work teaches you one thing. / You own the **outcome**, / and you can always show your **work**. ///

_This is your one resume sentence, and it is doing story work, not status work. It is the Flip template
(YC's own exemplar): the credential arrives inside the story and sets up the product's whole thesis.
Nine of 22 accepted videos recite no employer or school at all, so one is plenty._

### Beat 3 · the wall (0:30 to 0:48)

> Then ten years in product. /
> And when AI agents arrived, / something **broke**. //
>
> They did **more** of my work. / I could explain **less** of it. //
>
> Answerable for **everything**. / Able to prove almost **nothing**. ///

_This is the emotional centre. Every sentence is under eight words. "More / less" and
"everything / nothing" are the two hinges. Do not add specifics here that are not in your own experience._

### Beat 4 · what it delivers (0:48 to 1:08)

> So I built what was **missing**. //
>
> Supaprod reads everything your company already **knows** / and tells you what to build **next**. /
> Agents **ship** it. //
>
> And it **remembers**. / Every call, / the evidence behind it, / and whether it actually **worked**. ///

_Outcome, not features. No surface is named, no agent is named. This is the version you asked for:
storytelling about what it delivers._

### Beat 5 · the proof (1:08 to 1:20)

> **Eight** weeks. / Over **four thousand** commits. / **One** person, directing agents. /
> Every change on the **record**. //
>
> It's **live**. / Public launch in **September**. ///

### Beat 6 · the close — DO NOT CHANGE THIS (1:20 to 1:28)

> Agents do the **work**. / You **answer** for it. / **Supaprod is how you answer.** (down)

_Three sentences of 4, 5 and 6 words. Subject and verb in the first two words every time. It repeats
"answer" instead of reaching for a synonym, which is both easier to say and what makes the ear remember it.
It is the easiest line in the whole script to say under pressure, which is why it is last and why it is the
first thing you rehearse._

### One optional insert

Only if your stopwatch read lands at **72 seconds or under**, add this to the end of Beat 3's first line:

> Then ten years in product. / Most recently the AI platform **two hundred banks** build on. //

Ten words. It is your second resume sentence, so it is the first thing to cut if you are tight. It earns its
place only because it proves you have shipped AI product for customers who live and die on accountability,
which is the same thesis as the ISRO line.

## Your English is not the problem, and here is the evidence

You said you fumble. **At least 9 of 19 accepted videos have audible grammar errors, garbled words, or
founders talking over each other. Not one is polished.** Verbatim, from accepted applications:

- **Stilt** (accepted 2016, Indian founders, sustained non-native grammar): _"when both of us move to us a
  while back we didn't have any credit history in us because of that we are not able to get any credit card."_
- **Artisan W24** (accepted) announces on camera that it blew the clock: _"so we are definitely out of time
  but we're going to build a hundred billion company"_ — and its punchline is cut off mid-sentence.
- **Merse S24** (accepted): _"we are hungry down right now beaing and shipping from San Francis."_
- **Kater W24** (accepted) fumbles the handoff live, and says _"I a UCLA graduate."_
- **Lumona W24** (accepted) closes with an expletive left in.
- **Beulr S21** (accepted) spends 6 of its 64 seconds talking about his haircut.

A small stumble is **evidence you are complying** with YC's instruction not to recite. A flawless read is
evidence against it. Alex Lebrun, a French YC founder, on this exact worry: _"Speak slowly, articulate. It's
OK to have a strong accent (I do), but it makes it harder for the partners to understand what you say."_
And Paul Graham: _"if you don't feel you're speaking too slowly, you're probably speaking too fast."_

**Pace this at 125 words per minute, not 145.** 145 is the rate for the demo voiceover, where nobody sees
your face and you can re-take the audio over static footage. On camera, under adrenaline, 125 scripted gives
you headroom to drift up to 135 and still fit.

## The one-hour practice protocol

**The first five minutes are a rewrite, not a rehearsal.** Practising a sentence you trip on only makes you
fluent at failing.

| Time | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| **0 to 5 min** | **Rewrite.** Read the whole thing aloud once at normal speed. Mark every place your tongue trips. **Change the word. Do not drill it.** If you cannot say a sentence in one comfortable breath, it is too long. |
| **5 to 10 min** | **Stopwatch read.** Seated, full voice, script in hand, no camera. Target **75 to 80 seconds** for the 90 version, **52 to 55** for the 60 cut. If you are over, cut words. **Never speed up.** |
| **10 to 15 min** | **Three slow reads at half speed**, exaggerating every pause. You are building muscle memory for the pause positions, not the words. |
| **15 to 22 min** | **Audio only.** Phone voice memo, one take, eyes closed or at the wall. Then listen back once with headphones. **This is the highest-value fifteen minutes in the hour**, because it splits the two hard problems apart: the language, and being looked at. |
| | Listen for exactly three things: a sentence you did not finish as written, a place you ran out of air, a place you sped up. **Fix the script, not the delivery.** |
| **22 to 28 min** | **Second audio take.** It should be audibly cleaner. If it is not, the script is still too hard. Cut more. |
| **28 to 38 min** | **Three camera takes back to back. Do not review between them** — reviewing makes the next one worse. Bullet card taped **directly under the lens**, never on a second screen; eye travel is visible. |
| **38 to 46 min** | **Playback on mute first.** Check eye drift, hands, posture, dead face. Then watch the best one with sound. |
| **46 to 56 min** | **Two final takes with ONE correction.** One, not a list. |
| **56 to 60 min** | Pick, upload as Unlisted, paste the URL. |

**Warm up before take 1:** read any paragraph aloud twice so the video is not the first time you use your
voice today.

**Stop rule.** Stop when a take has the close said cleanly, no more than two audible stumbles, and is under
time. **Do not chase take eight.** After about five takes, energy decays faster than diction improves, and
energy is what partners actually respond to. Only two things must be right: **the first ten seconds and the
last three sentences.** Everything between them tolerates a stumble.

---

## What to do when you fumble mid-take

**Keep rolling** when: you trip on a word but the sentence finishes · you repeat a word · one "uh" · your
voice cracks · you lose a beat and pick up at the next sentence.

**Restart** only when: you say a wrong fact, number, date or name · the first ten seconds are dead · you skip
or mangle the close · you are over time · **you apologise out loud or laugh at yourself**, which breaks the
frame.

**Mid-take repair without restarting:** stop, one full pause, say that one sentence again from its beginning,
carry on. **Never say "sorry". Never say "let me start again". Never wave at the camera.** On playback, a
silent one-sentence restart reads as emphasis.

And do not restart because it _felt_ bad in the chair. The feeling and the tape disagree. That is what the
muted playback pass is for.

## The rule that keeps it storytelling, not spotlight

You asked for this specifically. It resolves grammatically:

> **Keep "I" for what you SAW, DID, and GOT WRONG. Cut "I" for what you ARE.**

"I hit this wall", "I built it", "I could explain less of it" are simultaneously the person and the story, so
they stay. Titles, seniority, tenure, employer valuations, hours worked are what you _are_, so they go.
"Senior PM at a two billion dollar fintech" is what you are. "The AI platform two hundred banks build on" is
what you did, and it is stronger.

**Budget exactly one sentence of pure resume.** One buys the right to be believed. Two starts to sound like a
job interview.

The tension does not resolve by removing yourself. YC's instruction literally begins _"introduce
yourselves"_, and partners are judging **how you think and talk** — whether you are specific, whether the
knowledge is first-hand, whether they would want to work with you. None of that requires the sentences to be
_about_ you. This script has your name in the first four words and exactly one resume sentence, and every
other line is about the problem and the outcome.

## Before you upload: six checks

1. Is it under time **on a stopwatch**, not an estimate?
2. Is there nothing in frame but you talking? No slides, no screen, no music, **no logo animation**.
3. Is every fact true today: the name, September, "it's live" and not "people use it", every number?
4. **Watched on mute:** do your eyes stay on the lens, and does your face move at all?
5. Did the last three sentences come out clean, with your voice going **down** at the end?
6. Could a stranger who watched it once say back what this company does, in one sentence?

**Kill-check:** is there more than one sentence about your job titles? If yes, cut back to one.

## Setup, in one list

- **Audio beats video.** Wired earbud mic, or your laptop's built-in mic in a soft-furnished room.
  **Not AirPods** (Bluetooth compression plus auto-gain pumping). Record ten seconds, play it back, then shoot.
- Sit close. Lens at eye height. A window or lamp **in front of you**, never behind.
- **Quit Slack and Mail outright.** Focus mode does not suppress in-browser or in-page notifications.
- Bullet card taped under the lens. Not on a second screen.
- No second person needed.
