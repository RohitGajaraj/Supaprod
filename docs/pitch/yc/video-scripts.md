# The YC videos — founder (≤1 min) + product (≤3 min)

> _Created: 2026-07-10. Two scripts, written to be SPOKEN — short sentences, your voice, no slides. Word counts sized to time (≈145 wpm). Assumes the sprint roadmap is built (the coherence cluster + launch rows); anything not yet true on filming day gets cut or reworded per the claim-on-wiring law — check [`../one-pager.md`](../one-pager.md) tags before recording. Direction notes in brackets are not spoken._

---

## Part 1 — the founder video (target 55 seconds, ~135 words)

**Format law (YC's own):** unlisted YouTube link, face clearly visible, ONE take, no production value, no script-reading eyes. Partners watch this to answer exactly one question: *would I back this person?* Energy and clarity beat polish.

[Webcam. Sit close. Look at the lens, not the screen.]

> Hi, I'm Rohit — solo founder of Cadence.
>
> For the last year I've been building software the new way: a fleet of AI agents doing the work, me making the calls. And I hit a wall every single day — the agents did MORE, but I could explain LESS. What did they change? Why? Was it right? I was accountable for everything and could prove nothing.
>
> So I built the missing layer. Cadence is an AI product team — it reads your customer signals, decides what's worth building, red-teams the bet, writes the spec, builds it to a pull request, and records whether the call was RIGHT. Every action has a receipt.
>
> Here's the proof it works: Cadence built itself. [hold up — beat] Three hundred shipped features, run through its own agent lanes, every one receipted — by one person.
>
> Agents do the work. You answer for it. Cadence is how you answer.

[Cut. Under a minute. Do not add a roadmap.]

## Part 2 — the product video (target 2:50, ~410 words)

**This is a narrative, not a demo tour.** One character (you), one day, one loop closing on screen. Screen-record the real product; voice over it. The camera never shows a feature the story didn't ask for.

**[0:00–0:25 — the cold open: the pain everyone knows]**
[Black screen. A Slack search box types: "checkout redesign decision".]
> Every product team has this moment. Someone asks — *"wait, why did we decide this?"* — and someone spends an hour digging through Slack, three docs, and a roadmap nobody trusts, looking for a decision that lives nowhere.
>
> Engineers got AI pairs and started shipping ten times faster. Which means product people now make ten times the decisions — with the same tools: documents that go stale the day you write them.

**[0:25–0:40 — the turn]**
[Cut to Cadence's Today. Calm. One column.]
> This is Cadence. It's not another place to write things down. It's an AI product team that does the work — and proves it.

**[0:40–1:20 — while you slept]**
[Scroll the receipts strip slowly. Point at a byline.]
> I run five products through it. While I slept, its agents read overnight signals from support, reviews, and analytics… clustered them into three themes… and re-ranked my bets — because an outcome landed on something we shipped three weeks ago, and Cadence noticed it under-performed.
>
> Every line here is signed by the agent that did it. Every one links to a receipt. This isn't a feed. It's a fleet, reporting for duty.

**[1:20–1:50 — the judgment lane: you make a call]**
[Open the top call. The Critic's evidence. Click through to a precedent.]
> My whole morning is three calls. Here's the first: the fleet thinks this bet is wrong — and it shows me why. The last time we bet on this exact premise… here's what happened. That's not an opinion. That's my own product's memory, arguing with me. I make the call. It's recorded — with the evidence I saw when I made it.

**[1:50–2:20 — decision to shipped, and the wrong path]**
[The spec generates. The prototype appears — on-brand. A PR opens; CI runs. Then: rewind.]
> Approved. Cadence writes the spec from the record, prototypes it in OUR brand, and builds — a real pull request, behind a gate no agent can cross. And when it gets something wrong? [one keystroke — the artifact reverts] One key. Reverted. Logged. Cadence keeps score on itself — it called eleven of the last fourteen. It publishes its misses. Ask any other AI tool for its error rate.

**[2:20–2:50 — the close]**
[Pull back to the portfolio switcher: five products, five stories, calls waiting.]
> One person. Five products. A team that never sleeps — and a ledger that proves what worked. Engineers got their revolution. This one belongs to the people who decide what to build.
>
> Cadence. Agents do the work. You answer for it. This is how you answer.

**Cut list if over 3:00 (in order):** trim 0:00 open to one sentence · drop the precedent click-through · shorten the prototype beat. Never cut the rewind/miss-record beat — it's the moment nobody else can film.
