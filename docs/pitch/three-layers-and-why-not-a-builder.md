## The answer to "what do you do", in three sentences

**You already have something that writes the code. What you do not have is something that decides
what is worth writing, watches it get done, and tells you whether it did what you said it would.**

Three layers, and each is the precondition for the next:

1. **Tell you what to build.** Evidence in, a decision out, with what we expect written down before
   the outcome is known.
2. **Run it end to end.** The decision becomes a plan, a design, a change, a deploy — handed to
   whatever builds for you, and checked at every step.
3. **Learn from it, then guide the next call.** The verdict lands against the expectation. What was
   believed, beside what happened.

**Layer 2 is where we meet the buyer. Layer 3 is why they stay.**

## Why we are not another Lovable, Replit or v0 — the strongest form of the question

This is the first question a good investor asks, and the honest answer is better than a defensive one.

### The market that generates code is finished and priced

Cursor ~$4B ARR · Lovable $500M · Replit $525M · Vercel/v0 $9.3B valuation · Cognition $492M
run-rate at a $25B pre. **Combined, over $48B.** Entering that is a model-quality war fought with
capital we do not have, on the one axis where being small is purely a disadvantage.

### The pain moved, and it did not move to generation

This is the load-bearing number and it is the whole thesis:

- **Code review time +441.5%** while throughput rose 33.7%.
- **Agentic pull requests take 5.3x longer to pick up.**
- **DORA metrics flat**, because output queued at review.

**Nobody is short of generated code. Everybody is short of confidence in it.** Every dollar of that
$48B made the problem we sell into worse. Their success is our market forming.

A practitioner named it before we did: *"PMs got faster at shipping but didn't get better at
defending why. The judgment gap got exposed."* And 82% report they are already faster with agents —
**so we sell relief, never speed. Speed is the thing that is hurting them.**

### Neutrality is the asset, and generating code would destroy it

Today Lovable, Replit, Cursor, Codex and Claude Code are **substitutable suppliers** to us. Their
commoditisation is our tailwind. The moment we generate code, every one of them is a competitor who
will not integrate, and a customer has to abandon the builder they already like in order to use us.

**The frontier converged on the same answer.** Codex returns a reviewable pull request, not a running
app. Linear made agents first-class assignees and integrates Cursor, Devin and Codegen rather than
replacing them. **The winning position above the build layer is the one that routes work to builders,
not the one that is a builder.**

### And what cannot be reconstructed

Everything else about a decision has been rebuilt from artifacts after the fact — Vercel's COO
reconstructed the true cause of a lost deal from Slack, email and call recordings with an agent built
in two days, running for about $1,000 a year. **So the record is not the scarce thing. The forecast
is.** What a team believed would happen, before the outcome was known, leaves no trace unless
something captured it at the moment of the call. Today nothing does.

**That is the one thing a competitor, an agent sweep over Slack, or next quarter's model release
cannot reconstruct** — and it is written at one station, which is why a route without that station
has no moat.

## What we say and what we do not

| Say | Do not say |
| --- | --- |
| "Decides what is worth building, hands it to whatever builds for you, and checks what actually happened" | "Runs the lifecycle" — it invites the comparison we just refused |
| "The forecast is captured at decision time" | Anything implying accumulated learning exists today |
| "Six of the seven stations have done their jobs in real runs" | "The loop is proven" — the honest query returns 0 of 93 |
| "The loop is wired and begins accruing on first real use" | Any present-tense claim about what the brain has learned |
| A number with its source and date | A time-savings figure. **None has been measured**, capture began 2026-08-10, and there are zero external users |

**Standing constraints:** no first-party user interviews have been run, and this is stated rather than
hidden. **The furthest honest run stands at ship.** Never describe the remaining distance as smaller
than it is — the four declines came from claims that did not survive a second question.

## The one thing that is still an open founder call

Whether we build a **sandboxed first-party build path**, so a run can complete and a stranger can see
a full loop with zero setup. The case for it is empirical, not aesthetic: **all five walls that have
ever stopped a real run sat at the handoff to somebody else's world** — a repo the product stopped
recognising, a merge gate no station was briefed to approach, dependencies a customer's repo cannot
install, a CI gate the builder disabled, and billing. Same cause five times.

**It is a fallback, never a competitor.** Argued in full in
[`../strategy/layer-2-build-question-2026-08.md`](../strategy/layer-2-build-question-2026-08.md).

## What would change this position

1. **A builder shipping layers 1 and 3 credibly.** Lovable or Replit adding a decision record with a
   forecast at commit time would collapse the distinction. Neither has, and neither is incentivised
   to — their metric is apps shipped, and a forecast slows shipping down. **But Notion shipped a free
   version of the lifecycle framing on 2026-07-09**, so this is the threat to watch and to name
   before an investor names it.
2. **The handoff proving unfixable.** If real customer repos keep blocking the loop after Build is
   briefed on all six steps, owning the builder stops being optional. That is measurable, and the
   five findings above are the instrument.
