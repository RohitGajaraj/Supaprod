# Logo concept directions

> _Created: 2026-07-04 · Last updated: 2026-07-04_

Four distinct marks for the product currently called Cadence (name in flux). Every SVG is hand-authored geometry, drawn in `currentColor` with no gradients, so each mark survives one-color print, dark UI, light UI, and an embossed favicon. All live in this folder; each was rendered and visually verified at large and small sizes during authoring. These are craft sketches, not final vectors: a designer should refine optical weights, but the geometry and idea are real, not placeholder.

A shared note on color: in the product palette, any of these can carry the glacier-violet-blossom thread gradient in hero contexts, with ember reserved for the "needs a human" accent. The sketches prove the marks do not depend on that gradient to read.

---

## 1. The Loom Knot

**File:** `1-loom-knot.svg`

**Idea.** One continuous thread tied into a woven trefoil: three lobes, three crossings, and the strand visibly passes over and under itself, so the mark is simultaneously a knot, a loop with no end, and a weave. It is the product thesis drawn literally: every decision is a strand that loops back through memory and binds the next one.

**Why it is ownable.** Nobody in the PM tool set owns a knot. Linear owns the offset circle, Notion the letterform, Productboard and Aha! own generic geometry, and Meta owns the plain infinity loop; a trefoil with true over-under weaving is mathematically specific, reads as craft rather than clip art, and encodes "compounding memory" in the topology itself (a trefoil cannot be untied without cutting the thread, which is exactly the moat claim). The construction is honest too: the SVG was generated from the actual trefoil curve, so the geometry can be regenerated at any weight.

**At 16px.** The three under-crossing gaps are sized at roughly 1.3x the stroke width, so at favicon scale the mark degrades to a clean three-lobed loop with visible braiding rather than a smudge; if needed, a favicon cut simply widens the stroke to 4.2 and keeps the same three gaps. Verified: still reads as a knot at 48px natural size.

**Improvement over the brief.** Instead of an infinity sign with a twist (which collides with Meta and reads as "forever" rather than "bound"), the trefoil gives three lobes that can be narrated as sense, decide, learn: the loop of the product itself.

## 2. The Facet Butterfly

**File:** `2-facet-butterfly.svg`

**Idea.** The existing butterfly equity, rebuilt from four flat cut planes: two large upper wings and two smaller lower wings, each a single sharp quadrilateral, with the unfilled vertical channel between the halves doing the work of the body. No outline, no curves, no gradient; the creature emerges from negative space, the way the product's judgment emerges from structured facts.

**Why it is ownable.** Butterfly marks in software are almost always curvy, cheerful, and consumer-coded; a faceted, instrument-grade butterfly with a negative-space body reads premium and slightly severe, which matches the dark "Loom" interface, and it retains recognition continuity with the current mark so nothing shipped needs a hard rebrand. The four planes also carry a story the old mark lacked: four facets, one spine of open space where the human sits.

**At 16px.** Four solid planes with a 3px channel collapse gracefully: the channel stays visible because it runs the full height, and the silhouette stays a butterfly because the mass ratio (large upper, small lower) survives rasterization; the favicon variant can nudge the channel to 3.5px and it still reads. Verified at 64px and 48px renders.

**Improvement over the brief.** Rather than just "sharpening" the gradient butterfly, this version makes the body itself negative space, which gives the mark a second read (an opening, a keyhole, a place for the person) and makes it reproducible in one color anywhere.

## 3. The Decision Path

**File:** `3-decision-path.svg`

**Idea.** The constellation reduced to its irreducible sentence: an open ring (the signal arrives, still a question), a thread down to a large solid node (the call gets made, and it has weight), and a longer thread rising to a second ring (the outcome comes back and is checked). The three nodes trace a shallow dip-then-rise, so the whole mark quietly draws the shape of a checkmark without ever being one.

**Why it is ownable.** Node-and-edge marks usually mean "graph database" or "org chart" because every node is identical; here the asymmetry is the meaning (open, solid, open; small, heavy, medium), which no competitor glyph carries, and the hidden-check gesture gives it the "receipts" story: decisions are not just connected, they are verified. It is also the only direction that literally diagrams the moat: decision plus outcome, joined by thread.

**At 16px.** Three dots and two short strokes are the most durable small-size vocabulary in iconography; the solid center node anchors the favicon, the rings simplify to dots, and nothing depends on line weight subtleties. This is the safest favicon of the four. Verified at 64px.

**Improvement over the brief.** The brief said three nodes and two threads; the upgrade is giving each node a different state so the mark reads as a story (question, call, receipt) instead of a network.

## 4. The Throughline (wildcard)

**File:** `4-throughline.svg`

**Idea.** Three thin strands converge on a small solid point and deliberately stop short of it; from that point, one bold line launches up and to the right, unbroken. Many options arrive, one call leaves: it is "the call" drawn as physics (convergence, commitment, trajectory) with no gavel, no checkmark, no lightning bolt.

**Why it is ownable.** Decision imagery in this market is either a checkbox, a fork, or an arrow; a weight-asymmetric convergence mark, where the thin strands never touch the node but the bold stroke grows out of it, is a drawing of judgment itself and does not resemble any competitor glyph (it is closer to a conductor's downbeat or a struck match than to analytics). The rule that makes it a system, not just a logo: thin means considered, bold means chosen, and that grammar can extend across the whole product's iconography and even its charts.

**At 16px.** The mark reduces to a dot with one heavy diagonal and three ticks; because hierarchy is carried entirely by stroke weight (2.2 vs 4.4), the favicon keeps its meaning even when the thin strands drop to a single pixel, and a minimal cut can drop to two incoming strands with no loss. Verified at 64px.

**Improvement over the brief.** The gaps are doing semantic work: unchosen options never quite reach the decision point, while the chosen line shares its exact origin. That over-under of touch and no-touch is the same honesty rule as the knot's weave, which quietly makes directions 1 and 4 a family.

---

## Recommendation

Lead with **1 (Loom Knot)** as the primary candidate: it is the most ownable, the most narratable, and it inherits the thread identity the brand already has. Keep **3 (Decision Path)** as the strongest favicon-first alternative and the natural in-product glyph language. **2** is the continuity play if renaming plus remarking at once feels like too much change; **4** is the sleeper that becomes stronger if the new name lands near "call," "line," or "commit."
