01 The tier retune: soft and faint stop reading as stain applies under every option below

Why the old tiers went muddy: the washes were alpha values composited over near-black. The background adds an equal gray to all three channels, which hits a saturated hue's low channels hardest, so ember's wash drifted to brown and blue's to gray slate. The fix keeps the tier grammar and swaps the two wash tiers to opaque hexes mixed along the hue line, one step brighter, so ambience reads as colored light instead of stain. Solids, dims, and borders are untouched.

Vellum is the archive material: what institutions wrote on when the record had to last. The serious challenger is no hue at all. The Geist school and Linear's refresh both argue machinery should be monochrome: the machine speaks in cool slate silver and its liveness is carried by motion, the pulse and the caret, not by color. Screens get quieter, and temperature alone separates the voices: warm is human, cool is machine. The honest cost: where agents work stops being findable by hue at a glance, the spine loses its blue fill in the approval signature moment, and silver has to fight the zinc text ramp for identity. Elegance, paid for with the glance.

04 Light mode: one identity per voice, calibrated per mode

Recommendation: one accent identity per voice, calibrated per mode. Same hue family in both modes, with per-mode lightness and chroma, exactly how ember and machine blue already flip in ink.css. The voice grammar is a language users learn once; per-mode hues would make the same chip teach two colors, and screenshots, docs, and demos would stop matching across modes. Every ramp on this board ships both calibrations in one token diff, so no candidate needs a second accent system to work in light.
