Warm machine, token set v1

Five hue scales, one 23 step neutral and two ink ramps. Every surface role and every ink role below points at a raw step, never at a bare hex, so light is generated from the same numbers rather than hand tuned. Shadows, scrims and tints are the only things expressed as rgba, because they are transparencies of a warm base rather than steps on a scale.

The law that keeps this from turning into a rainbow. A hue may never appear except to carry its meaning, and brass names exactly one thing per screen: everything brass on a frame points at the same waiting decision. Frames that are calm show four hues, frames with a diff show five, and neither is a licence to decorate. Count brass, not colours.

Umber, the warm neutral ground

Why it is warm. Red is always above green, green always above blue, and chroma stays under about three percent. It reads as a material rather than as an absence of light, and it never turns sepia because the chroma ceiling is low. Why 23 steps. Because both themes are built only from this ladder, including the light theme's near whites and the two dark steps that carry inputs and dialogs.

Type. Three faces, three weights, ten sizes. Nothing else ships.

Instrument Sans for all interface text, chosen for a humanist grotesque with slightly open apertures, which keeps 12px metadata readable on a warm ground. IBM Plex Mono for anything a machine produced: identifiers, timestamps, branches, diffs, terminal, and every number in a table. Instrument Serif appears exactly twice in the whole product: the sign in claim and the wordmark lockup. Three weights only, 400, 500 and 600, and b and strong are reset to 600 in the sheet so a stray tag can never reach 700. There is no bold and there is no size outside this list, which is why page titles are 26 and not 25, and gate titles are 22 and not 21.

Elevation. Warm shadow plus one pixel of edge light, always as a pair.

The shadow colour is not black, it is rgba(6,4,2,x), so the ground under a plate stays warm. Every raised surface also carries inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,246,232,.055). Shadow alone reads flat, edge light alone reads printed. Together they read machined.

Motion. Five curves, five durations, one rule.

Motion confirms, it does not perform. Only two things in this product are allowed to animate without a person acting: the ion sweep on a running progress bar, and the breathing dot on a live agent. Everything else moves because someone did something. Hover a card below to see each curve.

quietcubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1)

140 to 200ms. Hover, tint, colour, border. The workhorse.

entercubic-bezier(.16,1,.30,1)

240ms. Panels, popovers, a row arriving. Fast out, long settle.

exitcubic-bezier(.40,0,1,1)

120 to 140ms. Anything leaving. Departure is never slower than arrival.

confirmcubic-bezier(.34,1.28,.64,1)

320ms. A decision landing, a switch flipping. Used at most once per screen.

ambientlinear

1600ms loop. The breathing dot and the ion sweep. Never eases, so it reads as machine time.

RationaleWhy this, what it costs, where it strains

The idea is that the product should feel like an instrument you own rather than a document you visit. Pure black reads as a void and pure grey reads as a spreadsheet, so the ground is umber, a warm neutral where red sits above green sits above blue at very low chroma. Every surface is treated as a machined plate: a warm body, a translucent hairline edge, one pixel of light along the top, and a warm shadow underneath. That single material rule, applied without exception, is what carries the whole system, which is why the component list is short. On top of the ground sit five hues that are never decorative. Brass means a person is being asked for something, ion means a machine is working right now, verdigris means it actually happened, quartz means memory is speaking, rust means it stopped or was removed. A person can learn that vocabulary in one session and then read a dense screen without reading a single word, which is the point: the mission frame shows five agents in parallel and still answers what needs me at a glance, because only one thing on it is brass.

What makes it premium

Material, not decoration. Depth comes from an edge light and a warm shadow acting as a pair, never from a gradient wash. A real type scale. Nine sizes, three weights, no bold at all, and the tracking tightens as the size grows rather than staying at zero. Optical spacing over numeric spacing. A panel head is 13 tall, not 12 or 16, because a 15px title sits low otherwise. Restraint with a floor. Four hues on a working frame is the ceiling and also close to the floor, so nothing reads as grayscale. Motion that confirms. Two things loop, everything else moves only because a person acted.

What it costs to live in

Warm ground is easier for long sessions than a cold one, and the low chroma means it does not fatigue the way a tinted theme does after an hour. The gate treatment is the real ergonomic win: because brass is reserved, the eye stops looking for work, and the home frame answers what needs me in about two seconds rather than ten. The cost is discipline. The moment somebody uses brass for a nice looking chip, or ion for a static badge, the whole reading system degrades and every frame gets slower to scan. This direction is cheap to build and expensive to police.

Where it strains

Charts. Five semantic hues leaves almost no room for a categorical series palette, so any analytics surface needs a sixth, chart only, ramp derived from umber and ion, declared as out of the semantic system. Third party embeds. A GitHub or Figma iframe lands on warm ground looking cold and slightly blue. Light theme brand. Brass on paper needs the 500 step and still sits close to a caution yellow, so the light primary button leans darker than the dark one and needs a real accessibility pass. Screenshots. Warm surfaces photograph differently across displays, so marketing assets need a fixed export profile or the ground drifts pink.

What I would test first

The gate under load. Put three gates on Home instead of one and see whether the brass rule survives, or whether it needs a queue treatment where only the top gate is brass and the rest are quiet rows. Ion at scale. Twenty parallel agents means twenty sweeping bars, which may need a single aggregate pulse instead. The serif. Instrument Serif appears twice in the whole product, which is either a signature or an inconsistency, and only the founder can call it. Density on a laptop. The mission frame was drawn at 1000 tall, and at 800 the terminal is the first thing that should collapse to a summary line.
