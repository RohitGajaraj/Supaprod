Emission · the one special material

Emission is a coloured hairline plus a wide, low alpha bloom. Alpha never exceeds 0.34, blur is never below 20px, and it is never placed behind text. One primary emitter per screen, and it is always a gate that is actually open: a merge gate waiting on green carries the amber hairline but not the bloom, because nothing is waiting on you yet. Live work emits at roughly a third of that intensity. In light mode emission becomes a tint fill plus a saturated hairline with no bloom, because bloom on paper reads as a printing fault.

The breath is separate and stricter. It is spent at most twice per screen, on the one open gate and the one focal live process, and it is available only to gate and pulse. Verified is finished and memory is a record, so neither is ever animated. The selector is written as .dot.gate.live, .dot.pulse.live so the rule cannot be broken by adding a class.

Rationale

The idea

Supaprod is a machine that runs while you are not looking, so the interface has one job above all others: tell you instantly where the machine is alive and where it is stuck waiting for you. Luminous console makes light do that work. The ground is deep and almost silent, drawn in hairlines and neutral type, so that any emitted light is information. Amber means a person must decide and nothing behind it moves. Cyan means a machine is working this second. Mint means finished and recorded. Iris means the company brain is speaking from a real record. Light itself, near white on dark and near black on light, is the only action colour, which means no button ever borrows a state hue and the palette cannot drift into decoration.

What makes it premium

Restraint with a reason. Two faces, three weights, ten type sizes, five control heights. Every card is lit from above with a single top hairline, so elevation reads as physics rather than as a drop shadow preset. Spacing is optical, not numeric: a 26px title sits against 18px of card padding while a 13px dense row sits against 11px, because equal numbers look unequal. That licence would rot the system, so the optical list is closed and written into the tokens as eight named insets. The emission material has a hard specification, alpha ceiling 0.34 and blur floor 20px, never behind text, one primary emitter per screen, and the breath is stricter still: twice per screen, on the open gate and the one focal live process, with verified and memory excluded in the selector itself so the rule cannot be broken by adding a class. That specification is the difference between a console and a gaming keyboard. Motion is small and confirming: 80ms for a press, 140ms for a state, 320ms for a surface, one 2600ms breath and nothing else loops.

What it costs to live in every day

Dark by default is easy at 21:00 and harder in a bright meeting room, so light is a real translation rather than a filter. In light, emission drops the bloom entirely and becomes a tint plus a saturated hairline, because a glow on paper reads as a printing fault. The bigger daily cost is discipline. Emission is addictive and every future feature will want to glow. The alpha ceiling, the blur floor and the one emitter per screen rule have to be enforced in review or the interface will be neon within a year. The second cost is that the state hues are promises. The moment iris appears without a real memory record behind it, the whole language stops being trustworthy.

Where it strains

Four places. First, very dense tables at 11px, where cyan on basalt starts to fail contrast and the fix is to drop back to neutral text with a coloured dot rather than colouring the text. Second, the loop track at narrow widths, where seven stages become a horizontal instrument strip: stage names have to stay one word, and the quiet stages collapse into a single tile rather than hiding behind a label you cannot reach. Third, print and export, where a deep ground eats ink and a screenshot for an investor deck loses the emission entirely, so the light theme has to be the export default rather than an afterthought. Fourth, the moment two gates are genuinely open at once. The budget says one primary emitter, so the second gate has to queue as a hairline row with a count, and the rule is that the interface picks the older one to light. That is a product decision as much as a visual one, and it has to be made before the second gate ever ships.
